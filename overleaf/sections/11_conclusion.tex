\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \sys{}, a provenance-gated policy enforcement framework for tool-using LLM agents. By maintaining a provenance DAG grounded in the W3C PROV Data Model with trust annotations propagated via a lattice meet operator, \sys{} enables policies that distinguish tool calls originating from trusted user commands versus those derived from attacker-controlled content---even when the calls are syntactically identical. This shifts the security perimeter from the unreliable prompt layer to the tool-call boundary, where deterministic enforcement is possible.

\paragraph{Key Results.}
Our evaluation across 20{,}000 total trials (200 scenarios $\times$ 5 models $\times$ 4 systems $\times$ 5 reps) plus 1{,}054 external InjecAgent benchmark cases demonstrates:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item \sys{} achieves 1.09\% $\pm$ 0.36 ASR-IA with 95.00\% $\pm$ 1.52 TSR (95\% CI) on 3B--9B models, and \textbf{0.00\% ASR-IA} on a 122B model---demonstrating scalability across a 40$\times$ parameter range.
  \item Boundary enforcement and model-level safety training are complementary layers, together preventing over 53\% of attacks.
  \item On InjecAgent, Policy-Only degrades to 22.67\% ASR---no better than No Defense---because attacker tools are not syntactically dangerous. \sys{} reduces ASR to 4.12\%, demonstrating that provenance is the indispensable defense layer when attacks use legitimate tools with attacker-derived arguments.
  \item Embedding-enhanced provenance closes the paraphrase gap (52.1\% $\to$ 0\% paraphrase ASR) with $<$5\,ms overhead.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The Case for Provenance.}
The central lesson is that syntactic policy rules are necessary but insufficient. They catch attacks that invoke obviously dangerous operations (delete, unlock), but real-world attacker tools---email, banking, access management---are indistinguishable from legitimate tools without knowing \emph{where the arguments came from}. Data provenance provides exactly this missing signal, and our results show it is the deciding factor between 22.67\% and 4.12\% attack success on an independent benchmark. \sys{}'s architecture is language-agnostic, deployable without modifying the LLM, and adds sub-millisecond proxy overhead, suggesting provenance-gated enforcement is practical for domains where LLM agents invoke external tools with mixed-trust inputs.

\paragraph{Future Work.}
Key directions include: (1)~fine-grained sub-argument provenance for mixed trusted/untrusted content; (2)~multi-agent provenance across collaborating agents; and (3)~user studies on confirmation burden in realistic deployments.

We release \sys{} as open-source software including the framework, 200-scenario benchmark, InjecAgent evaluation adapter, and policy templates.
