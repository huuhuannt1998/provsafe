\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \sys{}, a provenance-gated policy enforcement framework for tool-using LLM agents. By maintaining a provenance DAG grounded in the W3C PROV Data Model with trust annotations propagated via a lattice meet operator, \sys{} enables policies that distinguish tool calls originating from trusted user commands versus those derived from attacker-controlled content---even when the calls are syntactically identical. This shifts the security perimeter from the unreliable prompt layer to the tool-call boundary, where deterministic enforcement is possible.

\paragraph{Key Results.}
Across five models (five vendor families, including GPT-4o-mini), five systems, five repetitions, and 1{,}054 external InjecAgent cases, \sys{} achieves 1.02\% ASR-IA with 95\% TSR on 3B--9B local models, and 0.35\% ASR-IA with 99.5\% TSR on GPT-4o-mini. The decisive result is the InjecAgent comparison: Policy-Only reduces ASR to 12.34\% through risk-tier assignments but still fails on Data Stealing attacks (21.7\% ASR), while \sys{} reduces overall ASR to 4.22\%---a 6.1$\times$ reduction versus No Defense and 2.9$\times$ versus Policy-Only---confirming that provenance provides essential additional security when attacker tools are legitimate tools invoked with attacker-derived arguments. An iterative multi-pass argument decoder achieves 100\% detection across 36 encoding robustness vectors, and an expanded adaptive adversary evaluation (50 white-box attacks across 5 bypass strategies, 180 LLM trials) confirms 100\% blocking---no adaptive attack bypasses the provenance mechanism.

\paragraph{The Case for Provenance.}
The central lesson is that syntactic policy rules are necessary but insufficient. They catch attacks that invoke obviously dangerous operations (delete, unlock) and reduce ASR through risk-tier assignments, but real-world attacker tools---email, banking, access management---include MEDIUM-risk channels where policy rules alone leave 21.7\% of Data Stealing attacks unblocked. Data provenance provides exactly this missing signal, and our results show it is the deciding factor between 12.34\% and 4.22\% attack success on an independent benchmark---a 2.9$\times$ additional reduction that closes the gap left by policy rules alone. \sys{}'s architecture is language-agnostic, deployable without modifying the LLM, and adds sub-millisecond proxy overhead, suggesting provenance-gated enforcement is practical for domains where LLM agents invoke external tools with mixed-trust inputs.

\paragraph{Future Work.}
Key directions include: (1)~fine-grained sub-argument provenance for mixed trusted/untrusted content; (2)~multi-agent provenance across collaborating agents; (3)~user studies on confirmation burden in realistic deployments; (4)~systematic confidentiality evaluation with ASR-C across all permitted exfiltration channels; and (5)~evaluation on larger proprietary models (GPT-4o, Claude).

The framework, 212-scenario benchmark, InjecAgent evaluation adapter, and policy templates will be released as open-source software upon acceptance; a DOI-bearing archival artifact will accompany the camera-ready version.
