\begin{abstract}
Tool-using LLM agents are vulnerable to prompt injection attacks where adversaries embed malicious instructions in content the agent processes. Existing defenses lack a fundamental primitive: \emph{data provenance}---without tracking where each tool-call argument originated, no defense can distinguish user-initiated operations from attacker-injected ones. We present \sys{}, a provenance-gated policy enforcement framework that maintains a W3C PROV-DM--grounded provenance DAG with a two-element trust lattice, resolves each argument's origin through a three-stage pipeline, and evaluates declarative policies at the tool-call boundary. Across \textbf{26{,}500+ trials} spanning five LLMs (five vendor families, including GPT-4o-mini), five defense systems, and five repetitions, \sys{} achieves \textbf{1.02\% ASR-IA} with \textbf{95\% task success}---a 24$\times$ reduction versus no defense. On the external InjecAgent benchmark (1{,}054 cases $\times$ 5 models $\times$ 3 reps), policy enforcement \emph{without} provenance reduces ASR to 12.34\% but still fails on data-stealing attacks (21.7\%), while \sys{} reduces ASR to \textbf{4.22\%}---a 6.1$\times$ reduction---confirming provenance is essential when attacker tools are syntactically indistinguishable from legitimate ones. An iterative multi-pass decoder achieves 100\% detection on 36 encoding robustness vectors, and an expanded adaptive adversary evaluation (50 white-box attack scenarios, 180 LLM trials) confirms 100\% blocking---no adaptive attack bypasses the provenance mechanism.
\end{abstract}
